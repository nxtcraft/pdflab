How to reduce the size of a PDF

Large PDFs are a common problem: an email attachment limit, a government or exam portal that accepts only a few hundred kilobytes, or a WhatsApp document that will not send. This tool shrinks a PDF by re-rendering its pages at a lower quality. You can pick a quality level (Low, Medium or High), or set a maximum output size in KB or MB and let the tool aim for that target.

Scanned documents and PDFs full of photos shrink the most, often by half or more. Text-only PDFs are already small, so there is little to gain. Because pages are re-rendered as images, the text in the compressed file is no longer selectable, so always keep your original.

How to use it

  1. Drop your PDF onto the upload area.
  2. Choose a quality level, or switch to Target Size and enter the maximum size, for example 200 KB or 1 MB.
  3. Press Compress PDF and wait while your browser processes the pages.
  4. Download the result and check the file size and readability before you upload it anywhere.

Tips

  • Low quality gives roughly 50 to 70% reduction, Medium about 35 to 50%, and High about 15 to 30%.
  • If the file is still too large, choose Low quality or split the document into two parts.
  • Do not compress an already compressed file repeatedly, because quality drops each time.
  • Open the compressed file and check that signatures, stamps and small print are still readable.

Frequently asked questions

How do I compress a PDF to 100 KB or 200 KB?

Open Target Size, type the number, choose KB and press Compress. Very long documents with many images may not reach 100 KB without becoming hard to read, so try splitting them.

Why is the text in my compressed PDF not selectable?

The pages are re-rendered as images to reduce size, so the text becomes part of the picture. Keep your original for copying text.

Is there a file size limit?

The file is processed on your device, so the limit depends on your device memory rather than a server.

Is my PDF uploaded?

No. Compression happens entirely in your browser.
